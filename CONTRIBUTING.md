# Contributing

Help make OpenHPSC easier to understand, build, and verify. Useful contributions
include a clear question, a primary-source reference, a reproduction report, a
design comparison, or a correction to these documents.

## Choose the right place

- **Discussions:** introductions, general questions, and ideas that need exploration.
- **Issues:** a bounded problem, investigation, or task with an expected result.
- **Pull requests:** reviewed changes to repository content.

Report documentation-site defects in the website repository. Keep project-wide
research and architecture coordination here.

## Propose work

An issue should state:

1. The question or problem.
2. Why it matters to OpenHPSC.
3. The intended output and completion criteria.
4. Relevant references, dependencies, and limitations.

Discuss significant technical changes before implementing them. Small corrections
can be submitted directly.

## Submit a change

Create a branch, make one focused change, and open a pull request. Explain what
changed and how it was checked. Use relative links for files in this repository.

For research notes, identify sources and distinguish observations, assumptions,
and conclusions. For experiments, include exact revisions, configuration, tools,
commands, expected outputs, and limitations. Do not describe a proposed test as
a completed result.

Maintainers review and merge changes. If a proposal becomes an accepted design
choice, record its rationale in `adrs/` and link the discussion or pull request.

## Licensing and attribution

By submitting material, you agree to license your original contribution under
the repository's MIT License unless a different applicable license is explicitly
identified. Submit only material you are authorized to share under those terms.

Retain third-party notices. Link to papers and external material rather than
copying substantial content without permission. Logo and branding rights are
separate from the repository license.

## Community conduct

Be respectful, constructive, and specific. Challenge technical claims with
evidence, acknowledge uncertainty, and help others reproduce findings. Keep
private information out of public issues, discussions, and logs.
